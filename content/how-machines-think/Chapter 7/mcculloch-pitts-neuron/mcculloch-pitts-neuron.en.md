# The Birth of Artificial Neurons: McCulloch and Pitts (1943)

In 1943, neurophysiologist **Warren McCulloch** and logician **Walter Pitts** published a groundbreaking paper that would lay the theoretical foundation for artificial neural networks. Their article, titled **"A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity"**, introduced the first conceptual model of artificial neurons.

## Historical Context

At a time when neuroscience was beginning to understand how biological neurons worked, McCulloch and Pitts proposed a mathematical abstraction that captured the essential behavior of these brain cells. Their model showed how networks of simple binary neurons could, in theory, compute any computable function.

## The McCulloch-Pitts Neuron

The model was revolutionary in its simplicity:
- Each neuron receives binary inputs (0 or 1)
- Inputs are weighted and summed
- If the sum exceeds a threshold, the neuron "fires" (outputs 1)
- Otherwise, it remains inactive (outputs 0)

This binary threshold model demonstrated that networks of such neurons could perform logical operations and, theoretically, any computation that could be described in finite terms.

## Impact and Legacy

While the original model was highly simplified compared to biological neurons, it established fundamental concepts that would later be developed by Frank Rosenblatt (perceptron, 1958) and eventually lead to modern deep learning. The McCulloch-Pitts neuron proved that computation could emerge from simple, interconnected units—an idea that remains central to artificial intelligence today.

## Original Article

The original paper can be accessed through various academic repositories and remains a foundational text in the history of artificial intelligence and computational neuroscience.
